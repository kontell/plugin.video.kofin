# S4.8 — SyncPlay fine sync through inputstream.tempo (run 2026-08-22)

Three Piers members on `jelly.konell.xyz` (Jellyfin 10.11.11), all 22.0-BETA1, kofin 0.21.0, inputstream.tempo 22.4.0, display clock **off** on every box: the desktop flatpak Kodi (**A**, x86_64, queue 4 s), the Galaxy Tab S5e (**B**, 192.168.1.150, armv7, queue 4 s) and the Bravia 4K AE2 "Sokoni" (**C**, 192.168.1.198, armv7, queue **16 s**, 50 Hz panel). Asset: the 10-minute 1080p h264/AAC timecode film from the inputstream.tempo rig, in the Recordings library as *SyncPlay Timecode (2026)* (direct play on all three). Driver: `tests/live/syncplay_fine_sync.py`, which acts as each member's own session against the server and samples positions over JSON-RPC at 4 Hz. Five runs; the numbers below are from run 5 unless stated, runs 1–4 being what shaped the code (§3).

**Verdict: PASS.** Every member is routed through the add-on, pulses land within a few percent of what they promise on all three platforms, a member perturbed by ±150 ms is back inside the deadband within one or two pulses with the others untouched, a 1 s step is closed by one seek plus one pulse, a group pause cuts a running pulse, and Kodi 22's queue goes 4/4/16 s → 1 s at join and back at leave.

## 2. Platform notes

- **Routing and audio.** `play <id> via DirectStream (tempo)` on every member; `Player.Process(audiodecoder)` = `ff-pcm_f32le` on all three, the Bravia included.
- **The Tab lands seeks badly.** A corrective seek on B restarts ~370 ms after it is issued and lands up to 350 ms *early* (MediaCodec cannot drop to the accurate-seek point: `demuxer seek to 13048`, clock restarted at 12.70 s), and the landing error varies by GOP position. The controller now learns what a seek leaves behind per device (§3) and finishes with pulses, so B converges, but it is the slowest member to do so — ~50 s from a bad start. A follow-up worth doing: when a device's measured landing error is large, prefer two saturated pulses (20 s, exact) over a seek for residuals under ~600 ms.
- **The Bravia** behaves like the desktop: accurate seeks (+594 → one pulse), pulses on target, no buffering with its queue cut from 16 s to 1 s on Wi-Fi over the 3½-minute run. Its panel stayed at 50 Hz throughout (`Display resolution ADJUST: 3840x2160 @ 50.000000`); with the display clock off that only sets cadence, not the rate.
- **Renderer rebuilds.** `CRenderManager::Configure` over run 4 (3½ min, ~8 pulses and 3–4 seeks per member): A 4, B 3 (1 `framerate changed`), C 4 (2 `framerate changed`) — the initial configure, seeks, and Kodi re-detecting 24.7 fps under a 3 % pulse issued soon after a seek, as the add-on rig predicted. No display-mode switch on any box. No `Traceback`, no `[ syncplay/buffering ]` on any member in any run.
- **Followers do not react to each other's pulses.** In every injection scenario the two untouched members issued nothing (B's one +82 ms pulse in run 5's inject-ahead window was its own residual, decided before the injection).

## 3. What the runs changed in the code

- **Run 1:** `Settings.SetSettingValue` answers a bare `true`, not an object — the queue had been shortened but recorded as not, so `queue_secs` was published as 4.0 and nothing was restored. Fixed in `kodirpc._call`.
- **Run 1:** `moved` read ~15 % low on every pulse. The add-on's `delta_ms` is the Δ it last *reported*, a queue depth behind the head; the head counters (`content_ms − output_ms`) are exact. `tempo.head_delta`.
- **Run 1:** the Tab pulsed ±50 ms against its own read jitter. Deadband 50 → 75 ms, window 2 → 3 s.
- **Run 2:** the Tab sat +400–600 ms behind after every corrective seek. The seek-cost estimate was measured against a 2 s landing tolerance, which a 400 ms correction satisfies before the seek is even processed, so it had decayed to ~150 ms while the Tab's real restart is ~370 ms plus a 350 ms early landing. Now `seek_lag_ms` = restart time − landing offset, timed from the clock restarting (`_clock_restart`), learned from every playing seek, used by both the scheduler's seek and `_align_after_resume`.
- **Run 3:** a seek that lands off-target left the member waiting out the 30 s seek blackout. Pulses now run inside the blackout, saturated at 3 % for 10 s.
- **Run 4:** two members measured +3.3 s after the group Unpause. My regression: the Unpause choreography seeks while still paused, and the clock-restart measurement polled a frozen clock for its whole budget before the resume went out. Measurement only for seeks issued while playing; budget 1.5 s.
